1Primal and dual

Associated with every LPP there is another LPP, called its dual (D). The original problem, in relation to its dual, is called the primal (P). Both are built from the same numbers $A$, $b$, $c$; solving one solves the other.

Definition: canonical (symmetric) form of the primal–dual pair

(P) Primal

$$\begin{aligned}\text{Max } &Z = c^{T}x\\ \text{s.t. } &Ax \le b\\ &x \ge 0\end{aligned}$$

(D) Dual

$$\begin{aligned}\text{Min } &W = b^{T}y\\ \text{s.t. } &A^{T}y \ge c\\ &y \ge 0\end{aligned}$$

$A$ is $m \times n$, $b$ is $m \times 1$, $c$ and $x$ are $n \times 1$, $y$ is $m \times 1$.

  • number of primal constraints ($m$) = number of dual variables;
  • number of primal variables ($n$) = number of dual constraints;
  • primal RHS $b$ = dual objective coefficients; primal costs $c$ = dual RHS;
  • the coefficient matrix is transposed: row $i$ of $A$ belongs to $y_i$, column $j$ of $A$ becomes dual constraint $j$.

Memory hook: "flip everything"

Max ↔ Min, ≤ ↔ ≥, rows ↔ columns, RHS ↔ costs. In canonical form, a Max problem has ≤ constraints ("a ceiling on resources") and its dual is a Min problem with ≥ constraints ("a floor on prices").

Example (class notes): the pair, solved both ways

Primal: Max $Z = 4x_1 + 3x_2$ s.t. $x_1 + x_2 \le 8$, $2x_1 + x_2 \le 10$, $x_1, x_2 \ge 0$. It is already in canonical form, so give the rows the dual variables $y_1, y_2$ and read the columns:

$$\begin{aligned}&\text{Min}\ W = 8y_{1} + 10y_{2}\\ \text{s.t.}\ &y_{1} + 2y_{2} \ge 4\\ &y_{1} + y_{2} \ge 3\\ &y_{1}, y_{2} \ge 0\end{aligned}$$

Added explanation: what the dual means

Think of the primal as a factory: $x_j$ = units of product $j$, $c_j$ = profit per unit, $b_i$ = amount of resource $i$ available. The dual asks: what is the lowest price $y_i$ I could put on each unit of resource $i$ so that the resources needed for one unit of every product are worth at least its profit ($A^Ty \ge c$)? The minimum total value of all resources, $b^Ty$, equals the maximum profit. So $y_i$ is the shadow price of resource $i$: how much extra profit one more unit of resource $i$ would bring. A resource left over (slack constraint) is worth $y_i = 0$, which is the idea behind complementary slackness (§6).

2Writing the dual of any LPP

The "flip everything" rule of §1 works only for the canonical forms (Max with ≤ and $x \ge 0$, or Min with ≥ and $x \ge 0$). Real questions mix ≥, ≤ and = rows with ≤ 0 or unrestricted variables. There are two routes, and they always give the same dual: (A) convert to canonical form first (the class-notes method), or (B) use the relationship table directly (faster in the exam).

Route A: convert to canonical form first (class notes)

For a Max primal, canonical form means every constraint is ≤ and every variable is ≥ 0. Fix whatever is not:

An = row: $Ax = b$Replace by two rows: $Ax \le b$ and $(-A)x \le -b$Two dual variables $u, v \ge 0$ appear only as $u - v$. Put $y = u - v$: the row's dual variable is unrestricted.
A variable $x_j \le 0$Substitute $x_j = -x_j'$, $x_j' \ge 0$Dual constraint $j$ gets multiplied by −1: its inequality is reversed (≤ instead of ≥).

Derivation for the ≥ row (class notes). Max $c^Tx$, $Ax \ge b$, $x \ge 0$ ⇒ Max $c^Tx$, $(-A)x \le -b$ ⇒ dual Min $(-b)^Tu$, $(-A)^Tu \ge c$, $u \ge 0$. Writing $y = -u$: Min $b^Ty$, $A^Ty \ge c$, $y \le 0$.

Derivation for the = row (class notes). Max $c^Tx$, $Ax = b$ ⇒ $Ax \le b$, $(-A)x \le -b$ ⇒ dual Min $b^Tu - b^Tv$, $A^Tu - A^Tv \ge c$, $u, v \ge 0$. Writing $y = u - v$: Min $b^Ty$, $A^Ty \ge c$, $y$ unrestricted.

Memory hook: SOB = Sensible, Odd, Bizarre

Label every constraint and every variable by how "natural" it is in its own problem:

  • sensible = what canonical form wants: ≤ in a Max, ≥ in a Min; variable ≥ 0.
  • odd = an equation; a variable unrestricted in sign.
  • bizarre = the wrong way round: ≥ in a Max, ≤ in a Min; variable ≤ 0.

The type never changes when you cross over: a sensible constraint gives a sensible dual variable, a bizarre variable gives a bizarre dual constraint, an odd one gives an odd one. Example: a ≥ row in a Max problem is bizarre, so its dual variable is bizarre: $y_i \le 0$.

Cases and traps

Case 2.1

Negative RHS

Leave it as it is. A negative $b_i$ just gives a negative coefficient in the dual objective (Example 4: $-8y_2$). Making $b \ge 0$ is needed only for the simplex method, not for writing the dual.

Case 2.2

A variable is missing from a row

Its coefficient is 0. Write the full coefficient block (Step "Transpose") so the columns do not shift.

Case 2.3

≥ in a Max / ≤ in a Min

Bizarre ⇒ the dual variable is ≤ 0. Alternatively multiply the row by −1 first; then the dual variable is ≥ 0 but appears with the opposite sign. Both answers are correct; the first is neater.

Case 2.4

Equality constraint

Odd ⇒ the dual variable is unrestricted in sign. Do not leave two variables $u - v$ in the final answer.

Case 2.5

Unrestricted or ≤ 0 variable

Free $x_j$ ⇒ dual constraint $j$ is an equation. $x_j \le 0$ ⇒ dual constraint $j$ has the bizarre direction (≤ in a Min dual, ≥ in a Max dual).

Case 2.6

Variable with zero cost

Still gives a dual constraint, with RHS 0 (e.g. $x_3$ in Mid 2026 Q3b).

Case 2.7

Constant in the objective

Max $Z = 3x_1 + 2x_2 + 5$: drop the 5, write the dual of the rest, and add 5 to the optimal value of both problems at the end.

Trap

Rows instead of columns

Dual constraint $j$ uses column $j$. Check the count: the dual must have as many constraints as the primal has variables.

Trap

Forgetting the sign conditions

"$y_1 \ge 0$, $y_2$ unrestricted, $y_3 \le 0$" is part of the dual. Leaving it out loses marks even when the rest is correct.

3The dual of the dual is the primal 2022

Duality is symmetric: either problem can be called the primal. So Dual(Dual(P)) = P, and Dual(Dual(Dual(P))) = Dual(P).

Theorem and proof

Theorem. The dual of the dual is the primal.

Proof. Take the primal in canonical form

$$(P)\quad \text{Max } c^Tx \ \text{ s.t. } Ax \le b,\ x \ge 0, \qquad (D)\quad \text{Min } b^Ty \ \text{ s.t. } A^Ty \ge c,\ y \ge 0.$$

To take the dual of (D), first write (D) in the canonical Max form. Min $b^Ty$ = −Max $(-b)^Ty$, and $A^Ty \ge c \iff (-A^T)y \le -c$:

$$(D)\quad \text{Max } (-b)^Ty \ \text{ s.t. } (-A^T)\,y \le -c,\ y \ge 0.$$

This is a canonical Max problem with matrix $-A^T$, RHS $-c$ and costs $-b$. Its dual (variables $z \ge 0$) is

$$\text{Min } (-c)^Tz \ \text{ s.t. } (-A^T)^Tz \ge -b,\ z \ge 0 \iff \text{Min } -c^Tz \ \text{ s.t. } -Az \ge -b,\ z \ge 0.$$

Since Min $(-c^Tz)$ = −Max $c^Tz$ and $-Az \ge -b \iff Az \le b$, this is

$$\text{Max } c^Tz \ \text{ s.t. } Az \le b,\ z \ge 0,$$

which is (P) with $z$ in place of $x$. ∎ The same holds for non-canonical forms, because the relationship table of §2 reads identically from both sides.

The dual of Max $4x_1 + 3x_2$, $x_1 + x_2 \le 8$, $2x_1 + x_2 \le 10$ was Min $8y_1 + 10y_2$, $y_1 + 2y_2 \ge 4$, $y_1 + y_2 \ge 3$. "Flip twice = no flip." Transposing twice gives back $A$; Max → Min → Max; ≤ → ≥ → ≤.

4Duality theorems

Throughout, the pair is in canonical form: (P) Max $c^Tx$, $Ax \le b$, $x \ge 0$ and (D) Min $b^Ty$, $A^Ty \ge c$, $y \ge 0$. The results hold for every primal–dual pair, because any LPP can be put in this form (§2).

Weak duality theorem 2022 · 2024

Statement

If $x$ is any feasible solution of the primal (Max) and $y$ is any feasible solution of the dual (Min), then

$$c^Tx \le b^Ty.$$

In words: every feasible Max value is ≤ every feasible Min value.

Proof

Since $x$ is primal feasible, $Ax \le b$ and $x \ge 0$. Since $y$ is dual feasible, $A^Ty \ge c$ and $y \ge 0$.

  1. $A^Ty \ge c$ and $x \ge 0$ ⇒ $x^T(A^Ty) \ge x^Tc$, i.e. $c^Tx \le x^TA^Ty = y^TAx$. (Multiplying an inequality by non-negative numbers and adding keeps its direction.)
  2. $Ax \le b$ and $y \ge 0$ ⇒ $y^T(Ax) \le y^Tb = b^Ty$.

Chaining the two: $c^Tx \le y^TAx \le b^Ty$. ∎

Corollary (class notes)

If $\bar x$ is feasible for the primal, $\bar y$ is feasible for the dual and $c^T\bar x = b^T\bar y$, then $\bar x$ is optimal for the primal and $\bar y$ is optimal for the dual.

Proof. For any primal-feasible $x$, weak duality with $\bar y$ gives $c^Tx \le b^T\bar y = c^T\bar x$, so no feasible $x$ beats $\bar x$. Likewise, for any dual-feasible $y$, $b^Ty \ge c^T\bar x = b^T\bar y$. ∎

This is the "check" used at the end of every answer in these notes: feasible + equal objective values ⇒ both optimal.

Two more consequences of weak duality:

  • Any feasible dual value is an upper bound for the primal maximum; any feasible primal value is a lower bound for the dual minimum.
  • If the primal is unbounded (Z → ∞), no dual-feasible $y$ can exist, since $b^Ty$ would have to be ≥ every Z. So primal unbounded ⇒ dual infeasible, and likewise dual unbounded ⇒ primal infeasible.

Strong duality theorem

Statement (class notes)

  1. If $\bar x$ is an optimal solution of the primal, then there exists $\bar y$ which is an optimal solution of the dual, and $c^T\bar x = b^T\bar y$.
  2. Conversely, if $y^*$ is optimal for the dual, there exists $x^*$ optimal for the primal with $b^Ty^* = c^Tx^*$.

Why it is true (the simplex proof, class notes)

Let the final simplex table have basis matrix $B$ and cost vector $C_B$. Put $\bar y^T = C_B^TB^{-1}$.

  • For a column $a_j$ of $A$: $Z_j - C_j = C_B^TB^{-1}a_j - c_j = \bar y^Ta_j - c_j \ge 0$ at optimality, so $A^T\bar y \ge c$.
  • For the slack column $e_i$: $Z_j - C_j = C_B^TB^{-1}e_i - 0 = \bar y_i \ge 0$, so $\bar y \ge 0$.
  • Value: $b^T\bar y = C_B^TB^{-1}b = C_B^Tx_B = c^T\bar x$.

So $\bar y$ is dual-feasible with the same objective value; by the corollary it is optimal. This also tells us where to find the dual solution: under the slack columns of the final table (§5).

Existence theorem (fundamental theorem of duality)

Statement (class notes)

  1. If the primal and the dual both have feasible solutions, then both have optimal solutions (and the optimal values are equal).
  2. If the primal (dual) has an unbounded solution, then the dual (primal) has no feasible solution.
  3. If the primal (dual) has no feasible solution, then the dual (primal) is either unbounded or infeasible.

Both infeasible really happens: Max $x_1 + x_2$ s.t. $x_1 - x_2 \le -1$, $-x_1 + x_2 \le -1$ (adding gives $0 \le -2$); its dual Min $-y_1 - y_2$ s.t. $y_1 - y_2 \ge 1$, $-y_1 + y_2 \ge 1$ (adding gives $0 \ge 2$).

Memory hook

"Optimal pairs with optimal. Unbounded pairs with infeasible. Infeasible pairs with unbounded or infeasible." Picture weak duality as a floor (Max) under a ceiling (Min): if the floor can rise forever, there is no room for a ceiling.

True or false? Mid 2025

  • "If the primal is infeasible, the dual is always unbounded." False: the dual may also be infeasible (example above).
  • "The primal and the dual can both be unbounded." False: an unbounded primal forces an infeasible dual (weak duality).
  • "The primal is optimal, the dual unbounded." False: if one is optimal, so is the other.

Using duality to show that an LPP is unbounded (class notes)

Question. Using the principle of duality, show that Max $Z = 4x_1 + 2x_2$ s.t. $x_1 + x_2 \ge 3$, $x_1 - x_2 \ge 2$, $x_1, x_2 \ge 0$ has an unbounded solution.

1

Dual. Both rows are ≥ in a Max (bizarre), so $y_1, y_2 \le 0$:

$$\begin{aligned}&\text{Min}\ W = 3y_{1} + 2y_{2}\\ \text{s.t.}\ &y_{1} + y_{2} \ge 4\\ &y_{1} - y_{2} \ge 2\\ &y_{1}, y_{2} \le 0\end{aligned}$$
2

The dual is infeasible. With $y_1 \le 0$ and $y_2 \le 0$ we get $y_1 + y_2 \le 0$, which can never be ≥ 4. So the dual has no feasible solution.

3

The primal is feasible. $x = (3, 0)$: $3 \ge 3$ ✓, $3 \ge 2$ ✓.

4

Conclude. By the existence theorem, an infeasible dual means the primal is unbounded or infeasible. The primal is feasible, so it is unbounded. (Directly: $x = (t, 0)$ is feasible for every $t \ge 3$ and $Z = 4t \to \infty$.)

Dual infeasible + primal feasible ⇒ the primal has an unbounded solution.

6Complementary slackness theorem

The fastest way to get one optimal solution from the other without running the simplex method: it turns the dual into a few simple equations.

Statement (class notes)

Let $\bar x$ be feasible for (P) Max $c^Tx$, $Ax \le b$, $x \ge 0$ and $\bar y$ be feasible for (D) Min $b^Ty$, $A^Ty \ge c$, $y \ge 0$. Then $\bar x$ and $\bar y$ are optimal for their problems if and only if

$$\bar y^T(A\bar x - b) = 0 \quad\text{and}\quad \bar x^T(c - A^T\bar y) = 0.$$

Componentwise, with primal slacks $s_i = b_i - (A\bar x)_i \ge 0$ and dual surpluses $r_j = (A^T\bar y)_j - c_j \ge 0$:

$$\bar y_i\, s_i = 0 \ \text{ for every } i, \qquad \bar x_j\, r_j = 0 \ \text{ for every } j.$$

In words: (primal constraint) × (dual variable) = 0 and (dual constraint) × (primal variable) = 0.

  • If a variable in one problem is positive, the corresponding constraint in the other problem is tight (holds with equality, "binding").
  • If a constraint in one problem is not tight (has slack), the corresponding variable in the other problem is 0.

Proof

From the proof of weak duality, for feasible $\bar x, \bar y$: $c^T\bar x \le \bar y^TA\bar x \le b^T\bar y$. The two gaps are

$$\bar y^TA\bar x - c^T\bar x = \bar x^T(A^T\bar y - c) = \textstyle\sum_j \bar x_j r_j \ge 0, \qquad b^T\bar y - \bar y^TA\bar x = \bar y^T(b - A\bar x) = \textstyle\sum_i \bar y_i s_i \ge 0.$$

Both are optimal ⟺ $c^T\bar x = b^T\bar y$ (strong duality and the corollary) ⟺ both gaps are 0. Each gap is a sum of non-negative terms, so it is 0 exactly when every term $\bar x_j r_j$ and $\bar y_i s_i$ is 0. ∎

Only one direction is forced

Slack ⇒ variable 0, and variable positive ⇒ tight. But a tight constraint does not force its variable to be positive, and a zero variable does not force its constraint to be slack (degenerate cases). Never write "tight ⇒ $y_i \ne 0$".

8Dual simplex method

Ordinary simplex keeps the solution feasible ($x_B \ge 0$) and moves towards optimality (all Zj−Cj ≥ 0). The dual simplex method does the opposite: it starts optimal but infeasible (all Zj−Cj ≥ 0 but some $x_B < 0$) and moves towards feasibility. No artificial variables are needed.

Idea (class notes)

Let the given LPP be Max $c^Tx$, $Ax = b$, $x \ge 0$. We do not insist that $b \ge 0$. The starting point of the dual simplex algorithm is a basic solution $x_B = B^{-1}b$ for which all Zj−Cj ≥ 0 but some $x_{B_i}$ may be negative: the solution is dual feasible but may not be primal feasible. The dual simplex method is essentially the same as solving the dual of the given problem by the usual simplex method.

When can it be used?

  • Typical case: Min problem, all constraints ≥, all costs ≥ 0. Convert to Max (costs become ≤ 0), multiply every ≥ row by −1 so a slack can start in the basis; the RHS becomes negative, and all Zj−Cj = −cj' ≥ 0. ✓
  • ≤ rows with $b \ge 0$ may be mixed in (Tut 2 Q4).
  • An = row: replace it by two inequalities (≤ and ≥) or use the two-phase method.
  • If some Zj−Cj < 0 in the starting table (e.g. a Max problem with a positive cost), the dual simplex method cannot be applied directly: use the two-phase method instead.
  • After adding a Gomory cut (Unit 4) or a new constraint, the table is optimal but infeasible: the dual simplex method finishes it.

Algorithm (class notes)

  1. Step 1. Start with a basic solution $x_B = B^{-1}b$ for which all Zj−Cj ≥ 0.
  2. Step 2. If all $x_{B_i} \ge 0$, stop: $x_B$ is optimal. If some $x_{B_i} < 0$, go to Step 3.
  3. Step 3 (leaving variable). Find the most negative $x_{B_i}$: $x_{B_r} = \min\{x_{B_i} : x_{B_i} < 0\}$. Then $x_{B_r}$ leaves the basis; its row is the pivot row.
  4. Step 4 (entering variable). Using only the negative entries $y_{rj}$ of the pivot row, find $$\frac{Z_k - C_k}{y_{rk}} = \max_j\left\{\frac{Z_j - C_j}{y_{rj}} : y_{rj} < 0\right\}.$$ $x_k$ enters the basis; its column is the pivot column. (All these ratios are ≤ 0, so "max" means the one closest to 0. Equivalently: smallest $\left|\frac{Z_j - C_j}{y_{rj}}\right|$, as in the second set of notes.)
  5. Step 5. Pivot exactly as in the usual simplex method and go to Step 2.

Infeasibility: if the pivot row has no negative entry $y_{rj}$, the LPP has no feasible solution.

(i) Dual of the dual is the primal

Let (P) be Max $c^Tx$ s.t. $Ax \le b$, $x \ge 0$. Its dual (D) is Min $b^Ty$ s.t. $A^Ty \ge c$, $y \ge 0$.

Write (D) as a canonical Max problem: Min $b^Ty$ = −Max $(-b)^Ty$ and $A^Ty \ge c \iff (-A^T)y \le -c$. So (D) is Max $(-b)^Ty$ s.t. $(-A^T)y \le -c$, $y \ge 0$.

Its dual, with variables $z \ge 0$: Min $(-c)^Tz$ s.t. $(-A^T)^Tz \ge -b$, i.e. Min $-c^Tz$ s.t. $-Az \ge -b$, $z \ge 0$, which is Max $c^Tz$ s.t. $Az \le b$, $z \ge 0$. This is (P). ∎

(ii) Weak duality theorem

Statement. If $x$ is feasible for the primal (Max $c^Tx$, $Ax \le b$, $x \ge 0$) and $y$ is feasible for the dual (Min $b^Ty$, $A^Ty \ge c$, $y \ge 0$), then $c^Tx \le b^Ty$.

Proof. $A^Ty \ge c$ and $x \ge 0$ give $c^Tx \le (A^Ty)^Tx = y^TAx$. $Ax \le b$ and $y \ge 0$ give $y^TAx \le y^Tb = b^Ty$. Hence $c^Tx \le y^TAx \le b^Ty$. ∎

Consequences. (1) If $c^T\bar x = b^T\bar y$ for a feasible pair, both are optimal. (2) Primal unbounded ⇒ dual infeasible, and dual unbounded ⇒ primal infeasible.

Mid 2025 · Q5a Existence theoremTrue / falseTrue or false, with reasons: (1) If the primal is infeasible, then the dual is always unbounded. (2) The primal and the dual can both be unbounded.

(1) False. By the existence theorem, if the primal is infeasible the dual is either unbounded or infeasible. Both infeasible happens: Max $x_1 + x_2$ s.t. $x_1 - x_2 \le -1$, $-x_1 + x_2 \le -1$, $x \ge 0$ (adding gives $0 \le -2$) has the dual Min $-y_1 - y_2$ s.t. $y_1 - y_2 \ge 1$, $-y_1 + y_2 \ge 1$, $y \ge 0$ (adding gives $0 \ge 2$). Neither has a feasible point.

(2) False. If the primal (Max) were unbounded and the dual had any feasible $y$, weak duality would give $Z = c^Tx \le b^Ty$ for every feasible $x$, a finite upper bound, contradicting unboundedness. So an unbounded primal forces an infeasible dual; "unbounded" needs feasibility, so both cannot be unbounded.

The primal has 4 constraints, so its dual has 4 variables but only 2 constraints, all ≥, with positive costs: a perfect dual simplex problem. Solve the dual, then read the primal solution from the dual's final table (the dual of the dual is the primal, §3).

1. The dual

$$\begin{aligned}&\text{Min}\ W = 10y_{1} + 6y_{2} + 2y_{3} + y_{4}\\ \text{s.t.}\ &y_{1} + y_{2} + y_{3} + y_{4} \ge 2\\ &2y_{1} + y_{2} - y_{3} - 2y_{4} \ge 1\\ &y_{1}, y_{2}, y_{3}, y_{4} \ge 0\end{aligned}$$

1

Standard form. Convert Min to Max by maximising $W' = -W$. Add a slack to every ≤ row.

$$\text{Max } W' = -W = -10y_{1} - 6y_{2} - 2y_{3} - y_{4} + 0s_{1} + 0s_{2}$$

subject to
