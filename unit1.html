1Optimization problems and the LPP

An optimization problem asks for the best value of an objective function over the points that satisfy a set of constraints. When everything is linear, it is a linear programming problem (LPP).

Definition: optimization problem (class notes)

$$\text{Max / Min } f(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n)\quad\text{subject to}\quad g_i(x_1, \dots, x_n) \le b_i,\ i = 1, \dots, m.$$

$f$ is the objective function, the $g_i \le b_i$ are the $m$ constraints, and $x_1, \dots, x_n$ are the decision variables. When $f$ and all the $g_i$ are linear functions of $x$ (of the form $\sum a_ix_i$, $a_i \in \mathbb R$), it is a linear programming problem.

General LPP

$$\begin{aligned}\text{Max / Min } &Z = c_1x_1 + c_2x_2 + \dots + c_nx_n\\ \text{s.t. } &a_{i1}x_1 + a_{i2}x_2 + \dots + a_{in}x_n\ (\le, =, \ge)\ b_i, \quad i = 1, \dots, m\\ &x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n \ge 0 \quad\text{(non-negativity restrictions)}\end{aligned}$$

Canonical form

Max: $\max \sum_j c_jx_j$ s.t. $\sum_j a_{ij}x_j \le b_i$, $x_j \ge 0$.

Min: $\min \sum_j c_jx_j$ s.t. $\sum_j a_{ij}x_j \ge b_i$, $x_j \ge 0$.

All constraints point "the natural way" for the sense of the problem; used in duality (Unit 2).

Standard form

Max / Min $\sum_j c_jx_j$ s.t. $\sum_j a_{ij}x_j = b_i$, $x_j \ge 0$, $b_i \ge 0$.

  • objective Max or Min;
  • every constraint an equation;
  • every variable and every RHS non-negative.

Needed for basic solutions and the simplex method (§5).

Matrix notation (class notes)

Min (or Max) $c^Tx$ s.t. $Ax = b$ (or $\le$), $x \ge 0$, where

$$c = \begin{bmatrix}c_1\\ \vdots\\ c_n\end{bmatrix}_{n\times1}\ \text{(cost / profit vector)},\quad x = \begin{bmatrix}x_1\\ \vdots\\ x_n\end{bmatrix}_{n\times1}\ \text{(decision vector)},\quad A = \begin{bmatrix}a_{11} & \cdots & a_{1n}\\ \vdots & & \vdots\\ a_{m1} & \cdots & a_{mn}\end{bmatrix}_{m\times n}\ \text{(coefficient matrix)},\quad b = \begin{bmatrix}b_1\\ \vdots\\ b_m\end{bmatrix}_{m\times1}\ \text{(RHS / resource vector)}.$$

Added explanation: the assumptions behind an LPP

  • Proportionality: each variable's contribution to Z and to every constraint is proportional to its value (2 units cost twice as much as 1).
  • Additivity: total = sum of the individual contributions (no interaction terms like $x_1x_2$).
  • Divisibility: variables may take fractional values (if not, it is an integer program, Unit 4).
  • Certainty: all the numbers $c_j$, $a_{ij}$, $b_i$ are known constants.
  • Non-negativity: you cannot make a negative amount of anything.

Hook: "PADCN" — Proportional, Additive, Divisible, Certain, Non-negative.

Example 4 (class notes): media selection, percentages in the objective

A sports company places adverts in magazines A, B, C to maximise exposure to principal buyers. Readers: 10 lakh, 0.6 lakh, 0.4 lakh; principal buyers among readers: 20%, 15%, 8%; cost per advert Rs 8000, 6000, 5000. Budget Rs 1 lakh; at most 15 adverts in A; at least 80 adverts in B and C together.

Decision variables: $x_A, x_B, x_C$ = number of adverts in A, B, C. Exposure per advert = readers × fraction of principal buyers: $10^6(0.20) = 200000$, $0.6\times10^5(0.15) = 9000$, $0.4\times10^5(0.08) = 3200$.

$$\begin{aligned}\text{Max } &Z = 200000x_A + 9000x_B + 3200x_C\\ \text{s.t. } &8000x_A + 6000x_B + 5000x_C \le 100000,\quad x_A \le 15,\quad x_B + x_C \ge 80,\quad x_A, x_B, x_C \ge 0\end{aligned}$$

As stated the data are inconsistent: 80 adverts in B and C cost at least Rs 4,00,000, far over the Rs 1 lakh budget, so this LPP is infeasible. In the exam, formulate exactly what is written; if you notice this, say "the model is infeasible as the data stand".

Example 5 (second set of notes): land, labour and fertiliser

A farmer uses all 50 hectares for potato and cabbage within 10 days. In a day he can plant only 4 hectares of potato or 15 hectares of cabbage. He has 5 bags of fertiliser; 1 bag covers 20 hectares of potato or 8 hectares of cabbage. Profit: Rs 180 per hectare of potato, Rs 240 per hectare of cabbage.

Decision variables: $x, y$ = hectares of potato and cabbage. Days used = $x/4 + y/15$; bags used = $x/20 + y/8$.

$$\begin{aligned}\text{Max } &Z = 180x + 240y\\ \text{s.t. } &x + y \le 50\ \ (\text{use "= 50" if the land must be used fully}),\quad \tfrac{x}{4} + \tfrac{y}{15} \le 10 \iff 15x + 4y \le 600,\\ &\tfrac{x}{20} + \tfrac{y}{8} \le 5 \iff 2x + 5y \le 200,\quad x, y \ge 0\end{aligned}$$

Clearing fractions (multiply by the LCM) keeps the numbers clean for the graph.

Cases and traps

Case 2.1

Profit not given directly

Profit per unit = selling price − (material + labour + …) per unit (Mid 2024 Q1a, Tut 1(A) Q8).

Case 2.2

Mixed units

Convert everything to one unit before writing a constraint (7 h 30 min = 450 min).

Case 2.3

"At least 50% must be single homes"

$x_1 \ge 0.5(x_1 + x_2 + x_3)$ ⇒ $0.5x_1 - 0.5x_2 - 0.5x_3 \ge 0$. Multiply out: never leave a variable in a denominator.

Case 2.4

Blending / mixing (percentages of a mix)

Let $x_j$ = fraction (or tonnes) of each ingredient; add the balance equation $\sum x_j = 1$ (or total) and one constraint per specification (Tut 1(A) Q6).

Case 2.5

"Between 30% and 40%"

Two constraints: ≥ 0.3 and ≤ 0.4.

Case 2.6

Many sources × many uses

Use double subscripts $x_{ij}$ (Example 3) and write one family of constraints for each limit.

Trap

Forgetting $x \ge 0$

Non-negativity is part of the answer; examiners deduct for it.

Trap

Constraint on the wrong side

"Not more than" = ≤, "not less than" = ≥, "no more than one-fourth of the total" = $y \le \tfrac14(x + y)$.

Result (corner-point theorem)

If an LPP has an optimal solution, then it has an optimal extreme (corner) point: at least one corner point of the feasible region is optimal. That is why checking the corners is enough.

One corner is strictly best (Example 1).

Case 3.2

Alternative optima

Two adjacent corners tie; the objective line is parallel to an edge. Write the whole edge as the answer (Example 2).

Case 3.3

The region is open in the direction in which Z improves (Example 3).

Case 3.4

Open region but Z gets worse in the open direction: answer at a corner (Min version of Example 3; Endsem 2025 Q1a).

Case 3.5

Infeasible

No common region (Example 4).

Case 3.6

Redundant constraint

A line that does not touch the region (e.g. $x_1 \le 20$ when $x_1 + x_2 \le 30$ already cuts earlier). It never changes the answer.

Case 3.7

4Geometry of LPP: convex sets

Why the optimum sits at a corner: the feasible region of an LPP is a convex set, and a linear function on a convex polygon is best at a vertex.

Convex combination
Of $x_1, x_2$: any point $\lambda x_1 + (1 - \lambda)x_2$, $0 \le \lambda \le 1$. These are exactly the points of the segment joining $x_1$ and $x_2$.
Convex set
A set S is convex if for any $x_1, x_2 \in S$, $\lambda x_1 + (1 - \lambda)x_2 \in S$ for all $0 \le \lambda \le 1$: the segment joining any two of its points stays inside S. (A disc is convex; a crescent or a ring is not.)
Extreme (corner) point
A point $x$ of a convex set is an extreme point if it cannot be written as a convex combination of two distinct points of the set (it is not strictly between two points of S).
Hyperplane
$\{x \in \mathbb R^n : p^Tx = \beta\}$ (a line in 2-D, a plane in 3-D).
Half-space
$\{x \in \mathbb R^n : p^Tx \le \beta\}$, one side of a hyperplane.
Polyhedral set
The intersection of a finite number of half-spaces.
Polytope
A bounded polyhedral set.

Results (class notes)

  1. $S = \{x \in \mathbb R^n : Ax \le b\}$ is a convex set.
  2. The feasible region of an LPP is a convex set (a polyhedral set).
  3. If $x_1^*$ and $x_2^*$ are two optimal solutions of an LPP, every convex combination $\lambda x_1^* + (1 - \lambda)x_2^*$ is also optimal. (So an LPP has 0, 1 or infinitely many optimal solutions — never exactly 2.)

Proof of result 1 (added)

Let $x_1, x_2 \in S$, so $Ax_1 \le b$ and $Ax_2 \le b$. For $0 \le \lambda \le 1$: $$A(\lambda x_1 + (1 - \lambda)x_2) = \lambda Ax_1 + (1 - \lambda)Ax_2 \le \lambda b + (1 - \lambda)b = b$$ (both weights are ≥ 0). So the combination is in S. ∎ Adding $x \ge 0$ (more half-spaces) keeps it convex, which gives result 2.

Proof of result 3: both give $c^Tx_1^* = c^Tx_2^* = Z^*$; the combination is feasible (convexity) and $$c^T(\lambda x_1^* + (1 - \lambda)x_2^*) = \lambda Z^* + (1 - \lambda)Z^* = Z^*.\ ∎$$

Which sets are convex? (class notes questions)

Method: sketch the set; if you can find two points of the set whose joining segment leaves the set, it is not convex. Intersections of convex sets are convex.

5Standard form: slack, surplus and free variables

Before using basic solutions or the simplex method, every constraint must be an equation with a non-negative RHS and every variable must be ≥ 0.

Slack and surplus variables (class notes)

  • Slack: for $\sum_j a_{ij}x_j \le b_i$, the non-negative variable $s_i$ with $\sum_j a_{ij}x_j + s_i = b_i$ is a slack variable (the unused amount of resource $i$).
  • Surplus: for $\sum_j a_{ij}x_j \ge b_i$, the non-negative variable $s_i$ with $\sum_j a_{ij}x_j - s_i = b_i$ is a surplus variable (the amount by which the requirement is exceeded).

Both get cost 0 in the objective.

6Basic solutions and basic feasible solutions (BFS)

The algebraic version of "corner points". The simplex method jumps from one BFS to a better one.

Definition (class notes)

Consider an LPP in standard form $Ax = b$, $x \ge 0$, where $A$ is $m \times n$ with rank$(A) = m$ ($m < n$). After rearranging the columns, let $A = [B, N]$ where $B$ is an invertible $m \times m$ matrix (the basis matrix) and $N$ is $m \times (n - m)$. Write $x = [x_B, x_N]$: $x_B$ are the basic variables (belonging to $B$), $x_N$ the non-basic variables.

$$Bx_B + Nx_N = b \ \xRightarrow{\ x_N = 0\ }\ Bx_B = b \ \Rightarrow\ x_B = B^{-1}b.$$
  • $x_B = B^{-1}b$, $x_N = 0$ is a basic solution.
  • If moreover $x_B \ge 0$, it is a basic feasible solution (BFS).
  • If all basic variables are > 0: non-degenerate BFS. If at least one basic variable is 0: degenerate BFS.
  • Maximum number of basic solutions $= \binom{n}{m} = {}^nC_m$.

Result: BFS ↔ corner points (class notes)

Every corner point of the feasible set S is a BFS of the standard form of the LPP, and conversely every BFS is a corner point. With no degeneracy the correspondence is one-to-one; with degeneracy several BFS (different bases) give the same corner point.

7The simplex method

Start at a BFS (the slacks), and keep moving to an adjacent BFS with a better Z until no improvement is possible. Works for any number of variables.

The simplex table (class notes)

  • Columns: $C_B$ (costs of the basic variables), $B$ or $X_B$ (basic variables), $b$ (their values), one column per variable, and the min-ratio column.
  • $Z_j = C_B^T y_j$ (cost column · column $j$), and the index row is $Z_j - C_j$.
  • Starting basis: the slack variables (their columns form the identity, linearly independent, with RHS ≥ 0). So simplex starts directly only when every constraint is ≤ with $b \ge 0$; otherwise use §8.

Algorithm for a Max problem (class notes flowchart)

  1. Find an initial BFS and compute all $Z_j - C_j$.
  2. If all $Z_j - C_j \ge 0$: the current BFS is optimal. Stop.
  3. If some $Z_j - C_j < 0$ and for that column all $y_{ik} \le 0$: unbounded solution. Stop.
  4. Entering variable: $Z_k - C_k = \min_j (Z_j - C_j)$ (most negative). Its column is the key (pivot) column.
  5. Leaving variable (min-ratio test): $\dfrac{x_{B_r}}{y_{rk}} = \min_i\left\{\dfrac{x_{B_i}}{y_{ik}} : y_{ik} > 0\right\}$. Its row is the key row; $y_{rk}$ is the pivot element.
  6. Divide the key row by the pivot; make every other entry of the key column 0 by row operations ($R_i \to R_i - y_{ik}R_r$). Go to 2.

Min problem: either use Max $Z' = -Z$ (done in all the tables here), or keep Min and reverse the signs of the rules: optimal when all $Z_j - C_j \le 0$; the entering variable has the largest positive $Z_j - C_j$; the min-ratio test is unchanged.

8The two-phase method

For constraints of type ≥ or =, there is no slack to start the basis. Add an artificial variable $A_i$ to each such row, first drive them all to zero (Phase I), then optimise the real objective (Phase II). Big-M is not in the syllabus; use this method even if a question says "Big-M" or "penalty" (the final answer is the same).

Procedure (class notes)

Phase I. Add artificial variables wherever required: in every ≥ row (after the surplus) and every = row; a ≤ row only gets its slack. Give each artificial cost −1 (Max form; cost +1 if you write Phase I as a Min), every other variable cost 0:

$$\text{Max } W = -A_1 - A_2 - \dots - A_l \quad(\text{equivalently Min } A_1 + \dots + A_l),$$

subject to the constraints in standard form, with the identity matrix in the basis. Solve by simplex until one of three possibilities occurs:

  1. Max $W^* < 0$: at least one artificial variable is in the optimum basis at a positive value ⇒ the LPP is infeasible. Stop.
  2. Max $W^* = 0$ and at least one artificial is in the basis at zero level ⇒ go to Phase II (first pivot it out, or drop its row if it is redundant).
  3. Max $W^* = 0$ and no artificial in the basis ⇒ go to Phase II.

Phase II. Use the optimum BFS of Phase I as the starting solution for the original LPP. Delete the artificial columns, assign the actual costs to the variables, recompute $Z_j - C_j$, and continue simplex until optimal.

Mid 2024 · Q4a TheoryWrite the conditions in a simplex table that show that the solution is (i) infeasible, (ii) unbounded, (iii) has alternative optima, (iv) optimal.

For a Max problem with index row $Z_j - C_j$:

  1. Optimal: every $Z_j - C_j \ge 0$ and every $x_B \ge 0$ (with no artificial variable in the basis at a positive level).
  2. Unbounded: some $Z_k - C_k < 0$ and every entry $y_{ik}$ of that column is ≤ 0, so no ratio exists and $x_k$ can increase without limit.
  3. Alternative optima: the table is optimal and $Z_j - C_j = 0$ for at least one non-basic variable; bringing it in gives another optimal BFS with the same Z (and every convex combination is optimal).
  4. Infeasible: the optimality test is satisfied (end of Phase I) but an artificial variable remains in the basis at a positive value ($W^* < 0$).

For a Min problem solved directly, reverse the signs: optimal when all $Z_j - C_j \le 0$, unbounded when some $Z_k - C_k > 0$ with column ≤ 0. Also: a basic variable equal to 0 ⇒ degenerate solution. See the table in §7.
